# Make the visual form serve this artifact

Honor the user's visual direction and an applicable existing design system. Otherwise, derive a direction from the reader, purpose and material. Those constraints leave room for expression; a subject does not imply one aesthetic. Inspect an obvious product theme or token file when matching an existing product.

## Decide what the design must accomplish

Separate the supplied constraints from choices you are free to make. Determine what deserves attention, what needs comparison or explanation, and how much information the reader needs at once. Compose these relationships before investing in decoration. Do not invent content to fill a familiar page structure.

Treat composition, type, imagery, surface and color as one design. Judge a choice by what it does for this artifact, not by whether it resembles a polished example. Different directions may satisfy the same brief. Familiarity and novelty are neither requirements nor proof of quality.

Use the subject's actual material when it contributes meaning. Keep visual emphasis proportional to the importance of the content. Supporting information should remain readable, and expressive elements should not obscure the principal task. Do not add sections or controls merely to make a small artifact feel designed.

## Resolve uncertainty with the smallest useful experiment

When an unresolved visual choice could materially change comprehension, operation or character, use a small rendered experiment to decide. Test the relevant content, state and viewing size before expanding the design. Compare alternatives when that comparison resolves the uncertainty; keep the essential reader task and content comparable so an option does not lose merely because it omits necessities. A representative fragment can be enough. Skip this work when the brief, existing design or a straightforward solution already settles the choice. Retain experiments that help subsequent editing, without making a fixed number of drafts or a process report a delivery requirement.

## Make relationships visible

Use scale, spacing, alignment, grouping and contrast to distinguish roles and show how content belongs together. Choose type size, weight, line length and rhythm deliberately. Do not apply Latin-style tracking to Chinese body text. Default to system fonts; a different licensed embedded face is optional when it materially helps. Canvas text follows the deterministic subset-font workflow.

Assign colors to the roles the composition actually needs and consider their area as well as their intensity. Local data/state colors need not define the whole identity. Derive the palette from the chosen direction, not an incidental example; neither banning hues nor rotating themes makes a choice appropriate. Support essential distinctions with text, shape or position too.

Preserve units, direction, association and evidential limits wherever the same information appears. Place qualifications where they affect interpretation, including summaries that may be read alone. Use the reader's language for labels and actions.

## Judge the rendered result

Read the artifact at its actual viewing size. Check whether attention goes to the intended content, relationships remain legible and the visual treatment suits the brief. When a choice fails, change the underlying hierarchy or representation before adding decoration. Stop when the task and intended expression work; more polish is not automatically more value.

Design a narrow-screen order deliberately. Preserve comparison, sequence and label/value associations when reflowing. If reflow would damage meaning, use a discoverable local scroll region instead of shrinking important content to illegibility. Check the state that imposes the greatest content or space demands, and keep useful controls reachable.

Set foreground and background explicitly. A coherent single theme is enough unless more is requested. Keep every supported theme complete across graphics and controls; HTML theme-state handling is specified in [html.md](html.md). Motion should clarify an observed change, respect reduced-motion preferences and leave essential content available at rest.
